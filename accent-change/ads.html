Hook

You know your subject better than almost anyone on the call. And you still get “sorry, could you repeat that?” two or three times an hour.

Your English isn't the problem. Your grammar is fine, your vocabulary is fine. It's a handful of sounds that slip the moment the call gets tense — and the listener stops hearing your point and starts decoding your words.

Body

I'm Dillon. I moved here from Munich to act, and for two years I got cast as Nazis and weirdos, because that's all a strong German accent is good for. So I spent two years in Voice & Speech training at the William Esper Studio and eighteen months drilling, and got my own accent to about 97% American.

Here's what nobody tells you: understanding how a sound works and keeping it under pressure are two different skills. You can watch a video on the American R today. Holding that R when someone pushes back on price — that only comes from drilling it, then getting pushed on purpose. That second half is what this workshop is.

What you'll learn

The three sounds that give away most foreign accents on an English call, and which one is costing you the most. A ten-minute drill you can run before your next meeting. And why speeding up to get past a hard word is the exact thing that breaks it.
