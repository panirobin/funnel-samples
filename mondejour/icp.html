01

Fears

What they're privately worried about. These never get said out loud in a DM, so the copy has to answer them before they come up.

"This is another guru"

They've seen a hundred men sell a way out. They assume the money is made from selling the course, and they're watching for the moment you prove them right. Building a clip live, on screen, is what answers it.

Heard in the wild
they allow way too many rip off artists call themselves Instructors who will overcharge and under deliver. Trustpilot review of Skool, 2 stars

"I'll post for months and never get paid"

Anyone who has looked into reaction videos has heard they get demonetized. They're afraid of doing ninety days of work for a page that gets flagged as reused content. The answer is specific: what commentary counts, shown on a real clip.

Heard in the wild
There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations and reaction videos. YouTube statement, quoted by Engadget, 14 July 2025

Getting stuck in a monthly charge

Money is tight, so a recurring fee they can't get out of is a real fear. Your calisthenics page says "No refunds", which makes it sharper. A one-time price and a plain guarantee remove it.

Heard in the wild
I wanted to cancel my membership but its impossible to do so or its so hidden. Trustpilot review of Skool, 1 star

03

Objections

Why they don't buy. The ones below came from men talking to you on stream, which makes them more honest than anything in a comment section.

"I've got no time"

True on paper. The honest answer is the screen-time test: most of them have three to six hours a day on the phone already. You're asking for one of those hours, and you say so.

Heard in the wild
I worked 16 hours yesterday. A man on your Times Square stream (YouTube, auto-captions)

"I've got bills"

They have an idea of their own and no room to risk anything on it. So the offer can't ask them to quit. It has to run beside the job until the page pays.

Heard in the wild
I wanted run my own lawn and landscaping business… I got lots of bills. A man on your Times Square stream (YouTube, auto-captions)

"The money takes too long"

Platform payouts arrive weeks after the views. A man who needs rent on the first won't wait through that without knowing it's coming. Tell them the timeline up front and it stops being a reason to quit in week five.

Heard in the wild
If it's October 1st and you make money, you got to wait all October and 21 days of November. Your Times Square stream (YouTube, auto-captions)
04

Buying triggers

The moment the problem stops being tolerable. Ads that name the trigger beat ads that name the solution, because the trigger is what he's already thinking about.

The week that breaks him

A 60 or 70-hour week, a paycheck that's gone by Tuesday, and the thought "I can't do this for forty more years". He's most reachable on the ride home.

Someone smaller gets paid

A guy from his school, or a creator with fewer followers than he'd expect, posts a payout screenshot. Envy turns "that's not for me" into "why not me".

Another birthday, same balance

A birthday, New Year, or a breakup. Any date that makes him count the years and see nothing changed. This is when the habits side of your message lands hardest.

05

Their vocabulary

The words this market uses for its own problem. Copy written in these words reads as one of them talking.

broke escape the top 1% first $1K reaction videos shift monetized payout cooked no excuses personal brand creator economy passive income

Struck-through terms are ones to avoid. They're marketer words, and this audience hears them as someone selling something.

06

Where they are

Where this audience already gathers, and what the tone is there.

Reels and the For You page

Short clips, late at night. You described their feed yourself: "Get your ish right. You're broke. You're poor. Get up. Keep going." They're used to being talked to hard, so soft copy gets scrolled past.

Live streams: Kick and YouTube

They watch you live and talk back in chat. A live training is a format they already trust, which is why it suits this audience better than a sales page.
