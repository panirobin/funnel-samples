01

Fears

What they're privately worried about. These never get said out loud in a comment — so the copy has to answer them before they're raised.

Buying the deal twenty other people bought

The fear behind every leads list and supplier list: by the time the stock checks in, the price has tanked because the same lead went to everyone. It makes them hesitate on good deals and overbuy "safe" ones that sit for months.

Heard in the wild
Too many sellers on the listing… everyone want to win the Buybox. Source — Trustpilot, 1★ review of Arbitrage Hero (a competing OA leads service)

Paying for sourcing that finds nothing buyable

They've paid for lists, tools or a VA and ended up with hours of scrolling and nothing that clears their numbers. So anything promising "more deals" gets met with suspicion — which is exactly why a webinar about the cash, not the deals, stands out.

Heard in the wild
Not enough good selling deals to make actual money. Source — review of an OA lead list, surfaced in search results

Getting stuck with stock they can't sell

Gated categories, IP complaints and listings that go wrong after the order's placed. The worry isn't losing a deal — it's the money sitting in a box in the spare room.

Heard in the wild
They were gated in too many of the products. Source — OAHunt, "Online Arbitrage Lead List Expectations"
02

Desires

What they'd actually pay for — stated the way they'd state it. More modest and more specific than a brochure would guess.

Profit they can see in the bank

Not a bigger revenue number in Seller Central. The real want is a payout that matches the effort, and knowing which SKUs are actually making it.

Heard in the wild
The business is about making money not winning the box. Source — comment on your Instagram reel, 18 Aug 2026

Getting past 10k a month

The number your coaching page already leads with. Below it, Amazon is still a side hustle; above it, they start thinking of it as a business. The block is rarely effort — it's capital and time.

Heard on your channel
They'd like to scale it, but they have really two big constraints… capital… and the other one is about the time. Source — you, "How to Scale Amazon FBA: 10k to 100k" (auto-captions)

Their evenings back

Most are sourcing around a job and a family. They don't want to work harder at Amazon — they want the hours they already give it to pay more.

Heard on your channel
Maybe they've got a family, and they're doing it on maybe a Saturday afternoon when they've got a few hours. Source — Eamon Kennedy, on your "10k to 100k" episode (auto-captions)
03

Objections

Why they don't buy. Taken deliberately from critical reviews and sceptical write-ups — the negative material is the most useful research in this document.

"It's just another list."

They've bought lists. Answer it by never selling the webinar as a list: it's the maths on stock they already own. The Top 500 only appears on the upsell, framed as "now you know which suppliers turn fast".

Heard in the wild
Every lead will meet my criteria. Source — OAHunt, listing it as the most common unrealistic expectation of lead lists

"OA is dead."

A question common enough that tool companies title articles with it. The honest answer is yours to give: you still buy OA every week, and thinner margins work fine when the stock turns monthly.

Heard in the wild
Is Online Arbitrage Dead for New Sellers in 2025? Source — Seller Assistant blog headline

"I don't have the capital to scale."

The objection that is really the whole pitch. Don't argue with it — agree, then show that the same capital turned three times does the work of three times the capital.

Heard on your channel
At the end of the day, we're all limited by capital, how much cash we've got. Source — you, "Your top-selling SKU might be your worst business decision" (auto-captions)
04

Buying triggers

The moment the problem stops being tolerable. Ads that name the trigger beat ads that name the solution, because the trigger is what they're already thinking about.

A disbursement smaller than the sales report

Sales look great, the payout doesn't. It's the moment "revenue doesn't count" stops being a slogan and starts being their bank balance.

A lead that tanked before it checked in

They bought it at a good price, it arrived at Amazon, and ten other sellers had got there first. Right after this, they're open to anything that isn't "buy more leads".

Deciding how hard to go on Q4

September and October are when they decide how much cash to commit to the busy season. A webinar about capital in September catches that decision while it's still being made.

05

Their vocabulary

The words this market uses for its own problem. Copy written in these words reads as insider; copy written around them reads as an outsider guessing.

OA leads ROI sell-through price tank gated Buy Box 10k a month capital refunds passive income dropshipping financial freedom

Struck-through terms are ones to avoid — they belong to a different corner of ecommerce, or read as get-rich-quick to a reseller who counts every pound of stock.

06

Where they are

Where this audience already gathers — useful for targeting, and for knowing which references will land.

YouTube, long-form

They watch 15–50 minute screen-share walkthroughs of SellerBoard, Keepa and Seller Central. Your profit-leak and top-SKU videos outperform your tool reviews — the webinar is that format, live.

Skool and Facebook seller groups

Where they ask "is this a good deal?" before buying. The tone is peer-to-peer and suspicious of gurus — plain numbers beat hype there, which suits how you already talk.
