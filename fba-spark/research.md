## The mechanism — in his words

This is the part worth getting right. Dave does not have a named, packaged framework the
way some coaches do; what he has is a **sourcing-efficiency system** that he states
explicitly and repeatedly. The whole funnel is built on it. His thesis in one line:

> "Sourcing efficiency is by far the number one skill you learn as you source products."

And the shape of the problem:

> "I've probably spent multiple thousands of hours just trying to find winning products."

Everything he teaches is about **eliminating listings fast** so the hours go into the few
that can actually work — then **keeping** the ones that work instead of starting over.

### 1. The Kill Criteria (his phrase)

Four non-negotiables. If any is present he spends **zero** further time on the listing —
he reads them off the Keepa chart in "2, 3 seconds" and moves on.

| # | Kill signal | What it looks like | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **IP complaint history** | Offer count falls off a cliff | "If there's been an IP complaint or if there's a history of it, it's most likely going to happen again." |
| 2 | **Brand on the listing** | Brand name in the Buy Box | "When the brand sells the product, it's just a matter of time before they actually IP you… it's a ticking time bomb." |
| 3 | **Major price spike** | Priced high today, historically low | A big seller holding MAP; "you don't really have control over these listings… this thing could drop literally tomorrow and then you're out money." |
| 4 | **Major offer-count spike** | Offers climbing fast in the last month | "This price is going to absolutely crash here." |

Note his own honesty about IP complaints — useful for the copy, because it disarms the
fear rather than selling it: *"We probably had well over 40, 50 of them by now on our
store. But it's not a big deal. You can beat them. It's just you don't want to stack them
up, especially when you're a new seller."*

### 2. The 10% Rule

> "If I'm not at least at a minimum 10% at the base price that I see on Google… I'm
> moving on from it right away, because no coupons, no discounted gift cards are even
> remotely going to get me close to hitting my threshold of about a 30% ROI."

The arithmetic he states: 10% at base + ~20% stacked savings (coupons, discounted gift
cards) ≈ the 30% ROI target. Under 10% at base, the discount hunt is not worth the time.

### 3. Keepa Product Finder as the only tool

> "You don't need any other tool to source besides Keepa Product Finder. This is how all
> of my sourcing frameworks work."

His actual filter stack, verbatim from the transcripts:

- **Not sales rank.** "Sales rank is so different in every single niche and category…
  something ranked 10,000 in games is not selling at all, but something ranked 10,000 in
  grocery is selling ridiculously quick." Use **Bought in the past month ≥ 50** instead —
  consistent across categories.
- **Buy Box current: from $X** — any number kills every **suppressed Buy Box** listing
  (no Buy Now button → no rotation → a new seller with no reviews gets no sales). Doubles
  as a minimum price / higher-ticket filter.
- **Amazon out of stock** — remove Amazon from the listing.
- **Categories** — include ones you want, or `is none of` to strip ones you don't
  ("I hate selling any types of clothing because it returns").
- **Title keywords** — brand + product word (`Ryobi` + `drill` cut 5,000 leads to ~500).
- **Negative keywords** — `-Owala, -Stanley` to strip the saturated stuff.
- **Total offer count** — minimum 4 (or 8) so you're not the only seller unless you mean
  to be.
- **Settings → Website → "show price history graphs as overlays" = yes** — so hovering a
  title in the results shows the chart without clicking in. This is the single setting
  that makes the scan fast.

### 4. Context clues

One winning lead is usually several. Variations — other scents, colours, sizes — priced
the same are profitable at the same buy price, so a good find gets multiplied instead of
banked once. Then the variation keyword ("pocket blush", "24 oz") goes back into the
title filter to pull the whole family.

### 5. The 15% rule (sellers vs velocity)

> "No more than 15% of sellers compared to how fast it sells."

Sells 300/month → max 45 sellers. 200 → 30. 100 → 15. Above that it's saturated and the
price is going to fall.

### 6. 30-day Buy Box ownership (the pink line)

Hover the pink line on the last 30 days. **Many different sellers rotating — especially
sellers with low review counts — is the buy signal.** A flat line is one seller
dominating the Buy Box: move on.

### 7. The Data → Offers tab

Where he confirms a listing actually sells and where he can price:

- Read individual sellers' stock drawdown ("30 down to 25 in about a week… about one a
  day") to get real velocity, because **Keepa's monthly estimate is usually too low**:
  "these guesses here are never correct, they're usually underestimated."
- Read the price **peaks**, not today's Buy Box — a slow mover with peaks at $67 is worth
  buying five or ten units of, even if it sits at $58 right now.
- Read **First seen** to spot replenishers (a seller who restocked the same ASIN three
  times), then click through into that seller's store to find more brands.
- **FBM-only listings with two-week shipping** are an opening: come in FBA with Prime
  delivery and take the Buy Box.

### 8. The sustainable model — the Vault

This is the real differentiator and the reason the whole funnel exists. He does not
re-source from scratch:

> "I don't ever buy products that I source. I rarely source for myself… I'd rather rely
> on my old leads. Those are tested. They're tried and true. If they're profitable again
> today and they went through a period of saturation, it's much less likely to get
> saturated today."

The machinery: Keepa **Track Product** alerts set at the price where the lead becomes
profitable again, plus a spreadsheet holding the buy link and the coupon, so the reorder
is a click and not a research session. His VA feeds the catalogue; he buys from
"my vault of products."

### 9. How he delivers

> "I don't leave you guessing. I implement them with you. So you actually are learning
> this and not just guessing watching a bunch of course modules. This is all done
> one-to-one."

## Audience

Two segments, and the funnel has to hold both without splitting:

**A — the beginner** who has watched the YouTube videos, opened Keepa, and is now staring
at 2,000 results with no idea which ones are worth a click.
**B — the stuck seller** who is buying, but buying badly: leads that tank, capital
locked in inventory that won't move, an account-health score they're nervous about.

- **Fears**
  - Sinking a weekend into sourcing and finding nothing buyable. Hours in, zero leads out.
  - Buying a lead and watching the price collapse before the units even go live.
  - Getting IP'd / suspended, or sourcing something they were never allowed to sell.
  - Cash locked in dead stock — the money is "in the business" but not moving.
  - That the whole model is dead and they're late to it.
- **Desires**
  - A repeatable scan they can run in an hour and come out of with real leads.
  - Leads that hold their price long enough to sell through.
  - Reorders instead of a permanent hunt — the same good product bought again and again.
  - First $10K profit; then a store that pays them monthly without more hours.
- **Objections**
  - "I've bought a lead list before — everybody gets the same list and the price dies."
  - "I've watched a hundred hours of free Keepa videos already."
  - "I don't have the capital to test."
  - "Is arbitrage even still viable in 2026?"
- **Buying triggers**
  - A specific lead that went bad — bought units, price tanked, money stuck.
  - An IP complaint or account-health warning landing in the inbox.
  - A full weekend of sourcing that produced nothing.
  - Watching a Keepa teardown, trying it, and getting stuck at the filter stage.

## Palette (designed, not extracted — see the egress note above)

Graphite ground + "spark" orange, which reads as the category (Amazon / Keepa chart
territory) without copying either brand's marks.

| Token | Light (`:root`) | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| `--paper` / `--paper-2` | `#FFFFFF` / `#F7F5F2` | `#0B0D11` / `#12151B` |
| `--card` | `#FFFFFF` | `#171A21` |
| `--ink` | `#16181D` | `#EFF1F5` |
| `--ink-soft` / `--ink-faint` | `#5A6069` / `#6F757E` | `#A6ADBA` / `#79808D` |
| `--brand` (panel colour) | `#16181D` | `#2E3440` |
| `--on-brand` | `#FFFFFF` | `#FFFFFF` |
| `--brand-ink` (eyebrows on paper) | `#333944` | `#C4CBD8` |
| `--btn-bg` / `--btn-fg` | `#FF8A00` / `#16181D` | `#FF9410` / `#16181D` |
| `--accent` (fills only) | `#FF8A00` | `#FF9410` |
| `--accent-ink` (accent as text) | `#9A4E00` | `#FFB45C` |
| `--on-brand-tick` | `#FFD9A8` (theme-fixed — sits on the graphite panel in both modes) | `#FFD9A8` |

Notes:

- The brand token is the **near-black panel**, so white type on the VIP ticket, the
  urgency band and the comparison column works with no rework. The orange lives in
  `--btn-bg` and `--accent`, so every button and every small fill is the spark.
- Orange as **text** never passes 4.5:1 on white (`#FF8A00` is ~2.4:1). Text uses go
  through `--accent-ink` `#9A4E00` (6.1:1 on white, 5.6:1 on `--paper-2`).
- Buttons are dark ink on orange: **7.5:1** light, **8.0:1** dark. White on orange would
  be 2.4:1 — do not flip it.
- The alert bar and the "Best value" flag hardcode `#3A1D00` on `var(--accent)` (6.6:1 /
  7.0:1) because the fill stays orange in both themes.
- Checked pairs, light: ink 17.8 · ink-soft 6.3 · ink-faint 4.6 · brand-ink 11.6 ·
  accent-ink 6.1 · button 7.5. Dark: ink 17.2 · ink-soft 8.6 · ink-faint 4.9 ·
  accent-ink 11.1 · white-on-panel 12.5.
- One known softness: in dark mode the brand panel sits only 1.4:1 from the card behind
  it, so the VIP ticket separates by its shadow and its orange flag rather than by
  luminance. Same trade-off the reference sample makes.
